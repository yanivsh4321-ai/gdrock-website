# Sales-call kit — agency call, 20 minutes

The whole call rests on one move: **you scan their client's site live and read the
score out loud.** Everything else is context around that moment. Do not open with
slides, do not open with pricing, and do not screen-share a dashboard full of your
own data.

## Before the call (5 minutes of prep)

1. Find **three of their client sites** from their portfolio page.
2. Scan all three at `gdrock.com` yourself. Write the scores down.
3. Open the console and **add those three domains** (`+ Add` next to the site
   picker). Leave it on the tab.
4. Have `offers/04-agency-portfolio.md` open on a second screen for the margin table.

Knowing the scores before the call is the difference between a demo and a
diagnosis. If all three score well, pick different clients or lead with the
portfolio-pricing angle instead.

## Run of show

### 1 · Frame it, 60 seconds

> "Before we talk about what I sell — I looked at three of your clients this
> morning. Can I show you what I found? It'll take two minutes."

Nobody says no to that.

### 2 · Scan one live, 3 minutes

Share your screen. Go to **gdrock.com**. Type their client's domain into the hero
field. Press **Scan my site**.

Say while it runs:

> "This is checking 14 things — whether there's a real consent banner, whether
> trackers fire before consent, whether the privacy policy names actual
> processors, retention, breach procedure."

Then read the score out loud and go through the findings. **Do not editorialise.**
The findings do the work; you sound like a consultant if you just read them.

Why this works: it is their client, their score, right now. It is not a case study
and it is not a claim.

### 3 · The four mistakes, 2 minutes

Scroll to the enforcement ledger. Do not read all eleven cases. Point at exactly
one:

> "Vueling — Spanish airline, €30,000. Same rule that hit Google for €150 million.
> Small company, small fine, identical mistake. That's the point: this isn't only
> enforced against giants."

That single comparison kills the "that's only for big companies" objection before
they raise it.

### 4 · Show how little work it is, 2 minutes

Scroll to the install section. One script tag.

> "This is the whole install. One tag per client site. I do the first batch with
> you."

### 5 · Open the console on THEIR client, 4 minutes

Switch to the console tab, already showing one of their domains.

> "This is what you'd hand your client. Consent logs, timestamped. Banner config.
> Their policy, versioned."

**Be straight about the empty state.** It will say *"No consent events recorded for
clientsite.com yet."* Use it:

> "It's empty because the tag isn't on their site yet. The moment it is, events
> land here. I'd rather show you an honest empty dashboard than a fake full one."

That sentence earns more trust than a populated fake screen ever would. Then switch
the picker to **gdrock.com** and show it full of real events:

> "Here's my own site so you can see it populated — 50 real consent events."

### 6 · The maths, 3 minutes

Only now do you talk money. Share the number, don't sell it.

> "You have roughly 40 client sites. Cookiebot is €30 a domain — that's €1,200 a
> month, and it went up in price last August. I bill the portfolio, not the domain:
> €499 a month for up to 50 sites. That's €10 a site.
>
> Agencies bill compliance on at about €45 a site. If you bill even 25 of your 40,
> that's €1,125 a month coming in against €499 going out."

Stop talking. Let them do the arithmetic themselves.

### 7 · The ask, 2 minutes

> "I'm doing a free 14-day pilot with the first five agencies — up to 20 of your
> client sites, no card. I onboard the first batch myself. At day 14 it either
> converts to a portfolio or you walk, and I'd want that agreed in writing now so
> neither of us is surprised."

The pilot is the ask. Not the €499.

## What to say about the gaps

They will find these. Say them first — it costs you nothing and buys the whole
call's credibility.

| Gap | How to say it |
|---|---|
| No multi-tenant back end | "Right now I manage the client list with you rather than you self-serving it. That's exactly what the pilot is for — and it means I'm doing the work, not you." |
| No case study | "You'd be the first. That's why it's free for 14 days and why you'd get me directly." |
| One-person company | "Yes. You get the person who built it, not a ticket queue. Test that during the pilot." |
| Company reg pending | Only mention if asked about invoicing. Have the real status ready. |

Never invent a customer, a number, or a feature. The console is honest now — keep
the pitch matching it.

## After the call, same day

Send one email, short:

1. The three scan reports as attachments.
2. One line of maths: their site count × €45 vs the portfolio price.
3. The pilot terms in writing: 20 sites, 14 days, free, converts or ends on day 14.
4. One next action with a date.

## For a store owner instead of an agency (10 minutes)

Same spine, smaller:

1. Scan **their** site live, read the score.
2. Point at one ledger case.
3. Show the one-line install.
4. Ask which they want: *"Do you want the documents to do it yourself — that's €29
   once — or do you want me to install the whole thing on your store in 48 hours,
   which is €249?"*

Two options, one question. Do not present four tiers to a solo store owner; it
stalls the decision. Care at €15/mo is the follow-up after Done-For-You, sold in
the same conversation: *"someone has to keep it current — that's €15 a month and
I'll set it up now."*
